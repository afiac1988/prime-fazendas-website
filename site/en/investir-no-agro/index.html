Why invest in agribusiness

A real, finite, productive asset — in an agricultural frontier that is still being consolidated.

Brazil is one of the world's great agricultural powers, and agribusiness accounts for a significant share of national GDP. Investing in farmland combines real-asset appreciation with income generation — provided the property chosen is technically sound and legally clean.

The thesis

Why farmland

Long-term appreciation

Farmland is a real, finite asset with a consistent history of appreciation tracking the region's productivity. In agricultural frontiers still consolidating, like Matopiba, the gain comes from appreciation plus production combined.

Income and production in the same asset

Unlike a purely speculative asset, the farm generates revenue — directly, through leasing, or through a farming partnership — while it appreciates.

Wealth protection

A real asset with low correlation to financial markets and a track record of preserving value through inflation cycles.

Legal security as a price differentiator

A property with a clean title, validated CAR (Rural Environmental Registry) and approved georeferencing sells faster, qualifies for bank financing, and is worth more than an equivalent area with pending issues. Regularity is value, not bureaucracy.

Attention

Regularity is what separates opportunity from a trap

An area with pending registry or environmental issues looks cheap and turns out expensive: it won't qualify for bank financing, it drags out closing, and it carries a liability that follows the property, not the previous seller. Every property in our portfolio is verified before it is presented.

Frequently asked questions

Common questions from people buying rural land

Can a foreigner buy a farm in Brazil?

Yes, within limits set by Law No. 5,709/1971, upheld as constitutional by Brazil's Supreme Court (STF) in April 2026. Brazilian companies controlled by foreign capital follow the same rule. Limits vary by area size and municipality — we review this case by case before any negotiation.

What are CAR, CCIR, ITR and georeferencing, and why do they matter?

These are the four documents that prove a rural property's land-title and environmental standing: CAR (Rural Environmental Registry) maps legal reserve and preservation areas; CCIR (Rural Property Registration Certificate) is the INCRA registration; ITR (Rural Land Tax) must be current; and INCRA-certified georeferencing defines the area's exact boundaries. Without all four, the property does not qualify for bank financing and the deal stalls.

Can you finance the purchase of a farm?

Yes, provided the property has regular documentation (see previous question). Rural credit lines and traditional bank financing require a clean title, validated CAR and approved georeferencing — which is why we audit documentation before listing any property in our portfolio.

How long does it take to close on a farm purchase?

It varies with the complexity of due diligence and the payment structure, but for a property with documentation already in order, the typical timeline from offer to deed runs 60 to 120 days, including document review, inspection and formalization at the notary's office.

What does 'price on request' mean?

It means the final value depends on variables confirmed at the time of negotiation — area availability, payment structure and any scope adjustments. We provide the price range and full details directly to genuinely interested buyers.

How does water availability and water-use permitting work?

Farms with surface water intake or wells for large-scale irrigation generally require a water-use permit issued by the relevant state or federal agency. We check permit status (or the potential to obtain one) as part of every property's due diligence.
