An irregular property is never cheap

Why acquiring land with registration, environmental or tax issues is a trap that costs far more than it saves.

When a property hits the market at 20%, 30% below market value, the first question should be: why? Nine times out of ten, the answer is: there's a problem.

The real cost of an irregular property

A registration issue (a deficient title, failed georeferencing, an inactive CAR) is not an administrative matter. It's a liability that travels with the property — it doesn't stay with the previous seller.

When you buy irregular land, you buy the problem too.

The roadblocks that show up later

Financing denied. No bank finances a property without clean documentation. That eliminates 90% of potential buyers. When it's time to sell, the door narrows.

Slower sale. The property takes months — or never leaves the market. Meanwhile, it sits idle. Interest, maintenance costs and lost opportunity pile up.

Permanently depressed value. Even after regularizing, the property is worth less because it became known as "the one with the problem." The market has a memory.

Environmental and tax liabilities. If the legal reserve has been encroached on, if the ITR was paid incorrectly for three harvests, you inherit the debt. INCRA, IBAMA and the federal tax authority don't forgive.

The math nobody does

Bought at a 30% discount? Savings: R$3 million.

Cost of regularization: R$500,000 to R$2 million (lawyer, surveyor, INCRA, documentation).

Time to regularize: 6 to 18 months.

Value lost from being on the radar of public agencies: R$1 million to R$3 million.

Financial cost of keeping the property idle: R$500,000 to R$1 million.

The actual outcome: You saved R$3 million and spent R$5 million. You lost R$2 million.

Properties in the Prime Fazendas portfolio are different

Every asset we present has gone through a full verification:

  • A clean title (registry search + legal opinion)
  • An active, SiCAR-validated CAR
  • INCRA-approved georeferencing
  • CCIR, ITR and tax documentation up to date
  • An analysis of environmental pending issues

It's not a guarantee against risk — it's the minimum. And it's rare in the market.

A regular property costs more up front. But it's worth infinitely more when it's time to sell.

The property that looks expensive is always cheaper than the one that looks cheap.

If you're evaluating a purchase

Be suspicious of a price well below market. Pay a lawyer for a registry search, a surveyor to check the georeferencing, an agronomist to evaluate the soil. It may seem expensive. It always costs less than getting it wrong.

And if you want to audit an asset's documentation before buying, talk to us. That's our job.
